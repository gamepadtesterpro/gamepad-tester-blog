Xbox controllers are the most common gamepads on PC, and they take plenty of punishment. A drifting stick, a trigger that never reaches 100%, or a bumper that only registers half the time can quietly ruin a game. A full check takes about five minutes, right in your browser, with no software to install.
## 1. Connect it with a cable
Plug the controller in with a USB cable for testing. A wired connection removes Bluetooth lag and dropouts, so anything odd you see is the controller, not the connection. If you normally play wirelessly, test that way afterwards and compare — a problem that only appears on Bluetooth is a connection issue.
## 2. Open a browser tester
Open a controller tester page, then press any button so the controller wakes up. A good tester shows every input live: stick positions, trigger values, button states and vibration. If no controller is detected, try another USB port or cable, or press the Xbox button once.
## 3. Check both sticks
Move each stick in slow, full circles. The dot should travel smoothly to the edges in every direction and return to the centre when you let go. At rest, both sticks should sit at zero — if the dot creeps while your thumbs are off, that is stick drift. A stick that stops short on one side at full tilt is wearing out.
## 4. Squeeze the triggers
Pull LT and RT slowly from untouched to fully pressed. The value should climb smoothly from 0 to 100 without jumping or sticking. A trigger that jumps from 40 to 90 in one step, or tops out at 80, will make driving games and shooters feel wrong.
## 5. Press every button
Work through them all: A, B, X, Y, both bumpers, Start and Back, the D-pad in all four directions, and press both sticks down until they click. Each press should light up instantly and go dark the moment you release. A button that stays lit after you let go, or needs a hard press, is on its way out.
## 6. Test the rumble
Trigger the vibration test and feel both motors. Both should buzz strongly and stop cleanly. Weak or one-sided rumble usually means a motor is failing.
## What to do with the results
If everything reads clean, your controller is fine — check the game settings or the USB port instead. If a stick drifts at rest, try a firmware update, then a gentle clean around the stick base; persistent drift means the stick module is worn. Note exactly which input is faulty before choosing between a repair and a replacement.
